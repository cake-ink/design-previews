Lime has no contrast on cream

You are right, and it is worse than it looks: lime on the cream card is 1.15:1, and the plain hairline already there is 1.49:1. A lime line is fainter than the border it would replace. On the dark card the same colour is 9.99:1. Lime is a dark-mode colour that happens to also exist in light. Four ways out, cheapest first.

What the system already says

DESIGN.md, the One Voice Three Signals Rule, your CAK-497 ruling: "Purple is the only hue that may carry interaction or selection… A signal never becomes a button, a link, a focus ring, or selection state." Going to Jordan is a selection. By the palette's own law the marker is purple, and the palette already ships the token for it — --acc-wash, "soft on-state fills". These are that answer.

Open times

Switch to Dark and tap again. The four seconds you are preserving measure 1.03:1 against the cream card and 1.79:1 against the dark one. The flash is already close to invisible in light — which is likely why it read to you as about a second rather than four. Whatever the settle becomes, the moment itself needs the badge to carry it, because the wash alone does not.

Why purple is the default here. Of everything light enough to read as a settle, --acc-wash sits furthest from the card at 1.19:1, ahead of card sand at 1.16 and lime 6% at 1.01:1 — which is nothing at all. It is also the one the palette already names for soft on-state fills, so the settle is in the hue allowed to mean selected while lime keeps the moment.

A fill can be this quiet; a line cannot. 1.19:1 across a whole row registers as a change of surface. The same value in a 1px border is invisible. That is the difference between this idea and the outline we started with, and it is why your instinct to make it a fade rather than an outline is the right one.

What to notice

  • Switch the frames to Dark and the whole question changes. Lime against the dark card is loud, and a 2px outline is plenty. Against the light card it is pale yellow on cream — the line is there, but it is the weakest signal on the screen. If the outline has to work in both, A and C are carried by dark and under-read in light; B and D hold in both, B because 3px of solid colour survives low contrast, D because the wash does the work the line cannot.
  • The flash is four seconds, not one — setJustAdded clears after 4000ms. It reads shorter than it is because 22% lime on cream is faint to begin with, which is the same contrast problem arriving early.
  • Only one row is ever marked today. The mark is row.starts_at === justAdded, so adding a second time silently un-marks the first. Any of these options drops that: every chosen time is marked, all the time, which is what "a clear differentiator" asks for.
  • Lime on cream is 1.15:1, and the hairline already there is 1.49:1. An outline in lime is literally fainter than the border it would replace. The 22% wash is 1.04:1. On the dark card lime is 9.99:1 and the same outline is excellent — this is a colour that works in one theme and not the other, not a thickness problem.
  • It works as a fill because of what sits on it. --on-signal on lime is 14.11:1, which is why the Added badge reads today while a lime line does not. On cream, lime has to carry dark content rather than be an edge.
  • Swapping purple out costs more than it looks. --acc is used 123 times in the studio stylesheet; it is what interactive means everywhere else. Lime is used 11 times. Make this pair lime and the open times become the one tappable control in the studio that is not purple — on the screen an artist uses most.
  • Lime is already carrying two jobs, and a third is landing. Today: the notice she may pass (.studio-notice, the calendar conflict) and celebration (the live dot, Day One). #1208 is extending the notice job across the studio right now. "A time still on offer" would be a third meaning for one colour, and it is the only one of the three that is not about attention at all.
  • The dash is doing the real work in the swap frames. With one hue, dashed-versus-solid is the whole distinction. That reads well at 2px and on dark; at 1px on cream the open pills nearly disappear, which is the same contrast problem that started this.
  • If lime stays on the chosen side only, nothing has to move. B is additive: a 3px edge on a row that already exists. No token changes meaning, the purple language is untouched, and the + mark keeps the contrast it has.
  • The Added badge is worth keeping either way. It is the only part that says just now rather than chosen. If the outline becomes permanent, the badge is what is left to carry the tap's feedback, and it already has a contrast-safe pairing (--on-signal on --lime, both themes).
